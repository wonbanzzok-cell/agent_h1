# Card News Plan: EV vs. Combustion Car, Who Wins After 5 Years?

- **Topic:** Electric vehicles (EV)
- **Plan:** #2 of 5. Total cost of ownership over 5 years, EV vs. combustion-engine (ICE) car
- **Created:** 2026-10-26
- **Format:** 8 cards, square 1080×1080 (Instagram). Card 1 is the thumbnail.
- **Target reader:** Value-minded drivers about to replace their car (30s–40s)
- **Key message:** The EV's higher price is paid back through charging, maintenance and tax savings. Whether that happens depends on where you charge and how much you drive.
- **Tone:** Friendly and number-driven, like a friend who did the math for you. Avoid hype for either side.

> **Note on figures:** Every number below is **indicative**. Most come from secondary sources (news and blog posts), and some are scenario assumptions marked *(assumption)*. Re-check fuel prices (Opinet), charging rates and subsidies right before publishing.

## Calculation basis (shared by all cards)

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Period / distance | 5 years, 15,000 km per year (75,000 km total) |
| Car class | Mid-size sedan/SUV |
| EV efficiency | 4.5 km/kWh |
| EV charging cost | Home slow charging 150–200 KRW/kWh → 33–44 KRW/km. Public fast charging about 347 KRW/kWh → about 77 KRW/km |
| ICE fuel economy / gasoline price | 12 km/L, 1,700 KRW/L → about 142 KRW/km *(assumption)* |
| Maintenance per year | ICE 500,000–800,000 KRW / EV 200,000–300,000 KRW |
| Annual car tax | ICE 1,600–2,000 cc about 290,000–520,000 KRW *(assumption, displacement-based)* / EV about 130,000 KRW flat *(assumption)* |

### 5-year running cost summary (indicative)

| Item | ICE | EV (home charging) | EV (fast charging only) |
|------|-----|--------------------|-------------------------|
| Fuel / charging | about 10.65M | 2.5–3.3M | about 5.8M |
| Maintenance | 2.5–4.0M | 1.0–1.5M | 1.0–1.5M |
| Car tax | 1.45–2.6M | about 0.65M | about 0.65M |
| **Total** | **about 14.6–17.25M** | **about 4.15–5.45M** | **about 7.45–7.95M** |
| **EV saves** | — | **about 10–12M** | **about 7–9M** |

(Units: KRW. "M" = million.)

## Card 1: Thumbnail

- **Main copy:** EV vs. combustion car: after 5 years, who actually wins?
- **Sub copy:** Car price, charging, maintenance and tax, all calculated
- **Visual:** An EV and a gasoline car facing each other, split screen, with a big "VS" and a scoreboard-style "5 YEARS" badge. EV side blue-green, ICE side orange-red.
- **Design note:** Main copy in bold, at most 2 lines. Leave the bottom-right corner empty for the account logo.

## Card 2: The question

- **Headline:** "EVs are expensive, aren't they?"
- **Body:**
  - The EV is still more expensive at the dealer.
  - But a car costs money every day you drive it.
  - We compared 5 years of driving at 15,000 km per year.
- **Visual:** Two price tags. The EV tag is bigger, with a "?" over it.
- **Design note:** Show the calculation basis in a small box at the bottom (5 years / 75,000 km / mid-size).

## Card 3: Round 1, the purchase price

- **Headline:** Round 1. Price: the combustion car wins, but the gap shrinks
- **Body:**
  - National subsidy plus the **new 2026 conversion bonus** (up to 1M KRW when you scrap or sell a combustion car)
  - Plus a local subsidy that varies by city or province
  - The more efficient the EV, the bigger the subsidy
- **Visual:** A price-gap bar shrinking step by step: base gap → after national subsidy → after conversion bonus → after local subsidy.
- **Design note:** Footnote: "Local budgets run out. Check your local notice first."

## Card 4: Round 2, fuel vs. charging

- **Headline:** Round 2. Cost per km: the EV wins big
- **Body:**
  - Gasoline: about **142 KRW** per km
  - EV, home charging: about **33–44 KRW** per km
  - EV, public fast charging: about **77 KRW** per km
  - Over 5 years: about 10.65M vs. 2.5–5.8M KRW
- **Visual:** Three horizontal bars (gasoline / fast charging / home charging) with coins piled on each.
- **Design note:** Put the big number "about 3–4 times cheaper" at the top.

## Card 5: Round 3, maintenance

- **Headline:** Round 3. Maintenance: no engine oil, fewer bills
- **Body:**
  - The EV has no engine oil, transmission oil, or fuel and air filters to replace
  - Yearly maintenance: ICE 500,000–800,000 vs. EV 200,000–300,000 KRW
  - Over 5 years: about 2.5–4.0M vs. 1.0–1.5M KRW
- **Visual:** A checklist of consumables. The ICE side is all ticked; most items on the EV side are crossed out.
- **Design note:** Footnote that tires wear a little faster on EVs because they are heavier.

## Card 6: Round 4, tax and perks

- **Headline:** Round 4. Tax: the EV wins again
- **Body:**
  - Annual car tax: ICE (1,600–2,000 cc) about 290,000–520,000 vs. EV about 130,000 KRW
  - Acquisition tax reduction and highway toll discounts for EVs
  - Over 5 years: about 1.45–2.6M vs. 0.65M KRW
- **Visual:** Two tax bills side by side. The EV bill is much thinner.
- **Design note:** Footnote that tax-break rules can change, so check the latest rules before buying.

## Card 7: The hidden variables

- **Headline:** Wait! These two decide the winner
- **Body:**
  - **Where you charge:** With only public fast charging, the saving drops from about 10–12M to about 7–9M KRW
  - **Resale value:** EV prices fall faster, and battery health (SOH) drives the used price. Check the battery warranty (usually 8 years / 160,000 km)
  - **How much you drive:** The less you drive, the slower the EV pays back
- **Visual:** Three warning icons (plug, price tag going down, odometer).
- **Design note:** Use a yellow "caution" accent so this card stands apart from the score cards.

## Card 8: Result and call to action

- **Headline:** Final score: EV 3 : 1 combustion car
- **Body:**
  - 5-year running cost: the EV saves about **7–12M KRW** *(indicative)*
  - Home charging plus long yearly distance means a clear EV win
  - Fast charging only plus short distance means look again before you buy
- **CTA:** "How far do you drive each year? Tell us in the comments, and save this post for when you replace your car!"
- **Visual:** Scoreboard (EV 3 : 1 ICE) with a summary table below it.
- **Design note:** Put the source line and "Figures are indicative; based on October 2026" in small text at the bottom.
