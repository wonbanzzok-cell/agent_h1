## Author profile

- Born 27 November 1970 in Gwangju, South Korea.
- Debuted as a poet in 1993 (five poems, including "Winter in Seoul", in *Literature and Society*). Debuted in fiction in 1994 when "The Scarlet Anchor" won the *Seoul Shinmun* New Year's Literary Contest.
- Won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Literature "for her intense poetic prose that confronts historical traumas and exposes the fragility of human life". She is the first Korean laureate and the first Asian woman to win the literature prize.

## Major works

| Work (Korean title) | English title | Korean pub. | English translation | Key awards / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 채식주의자 | The Vegetarian | 2007 | 2015, Deborah Smith | International Booker Prize 2016. Part 2, "Mongolian Mark", won the Yi Sang Literary Award in 2005. Three-part novel about a woman who stops eating meat. |
| 희랍어 시간 | Greek Lessons | 2011 | 2023, Deborah Smith & Emily Yae Won | A Greek teacher who is losing his sight and a woman who has lost her speech. |
| 소년이 온다 | Human Acts | 2014 | 2016, Deborah Smith | Manhae Literary Award 2014; Malaparte Prize 2017 (Italy). Set during the May 1980 Gwangju Uprising; follows the boy Dong-ho and people around him. |
| 흰 | The White Book | 2016 | 2017, Deborah Smith | Shortlisted for the International Booker Prize 2018. A meditation on white things and on the elder sister who died hours after birth. |
| 작별하지 않는다 | We Do Not Part | 2021 | 2025, Emily Yae Won & Paige Aniyah Morris | Kim Man-jung and Daesan literary awards 2022; Prix Médicis étranger 2023 (France); Émile Guimet Prize 2024. Friendship of two women and the Jeju 4·3 massacre. Han called it directly connected with *Human Acts*. |
| 빛과 실 | Light and Thread | 2025 | — | Essay collection including her Nobel lecture (see the Kyobo note). |
